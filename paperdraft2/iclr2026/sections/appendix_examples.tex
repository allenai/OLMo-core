We show one real example for every task in the suite (Table~\ref{tab:suite}), in the order of that
table. All content is verbatim from the eval splits. To keep each example readable we display only
3--5 documents (the gold ones plus a few representative fillers), truncate document text to
$\sim$190 characters, and elide the rest of the corpus with a
\texttt{[$\cdot$ more documents omitted]} marker; the real corpus sizes are stated per example.
Document IDs are 1-indexed, exactly as the model sees them. Each example also states the task
instruction, which is fixed per task and is what determines the output format the grader parses.

\subsubsection{BEIR FiQA (cross-encoder--cleaned negatives)}

\textbf{Instruction:} \emph{multi-document retrieval.}\\
\textbf{Query:} \emph{``Where should I park my rainy-day / emergency fund?''}\\
\textbf{Context (3 of 20 documents shown; 5 golds in this example):}
\begin{quote}\small
\texttt{[2]} \emph{(gold)} ``I would suggest your local credit union or local bank for security and
liquidity. Liquidity is probably the most important issue for a emergency fund.''\\
\texttt{[8]} \emph{(gold)} ``This is probably a good time to note that credit is not a liquid asset,
and not an emergency fund. Credit can be revoked or denied at any time $\dots$''\\
\texttt{[10]} \emph{(gold)} ``First off, you generally want to park your emergency fund somewhere
that is `safe', meaning something that is not subject to market fluctuations $\dots$''\\
\texttt{[$\cdot$ 17 more documents omitted: cross-encoder-surviving hard negatives ($\sim$10\% of
slots) plus random real corpus documents]}
\end{quote}
\textbf{Gold doc IDs:} \texttt{[2, 8, 10, 12, 19]}. FiQA is sparsely judged, so candidates scoring
close to the gold under the cross-encoder are dropped rather than used as negatives --- they are
likely unlabeled positives.

\subsubsection{MS MARCO (retrieval)}

\textbf{Instruction:} \emph{single-document retrieval.}\\
\textbf{Query:} \emph{``what is the difference between fables and folktales? for kids''}\\
\textbf{Context (3 of 20 passages shown):}
\begin{quote}\small
\texttt{[2]} \emph{(gold)} ``Folktale vs Fable. Folktales and fables can be understood as two
different types of stories that show a difference between them $\dots$''\\
\texttt{[1]} \emph{(hard negative)} ``Your job is to make sense of the fact or example in the context
of the overall main idea that is being conveyed $\dots$''\\
\texttt{[3]} \emph{(random fill)} ``Cascamite is a waterproof glue and is probably the must effective
glue of all $\dots$''\\
\texttt{[$\cdot$ 17 more documents omitted]}
\end{quote}
\textbf{Gold doc ID:} \texttt{2}. Hard and random passages are drawn from the same index, so they are
format-identical: there is no stylistic cue distinguishing a mined negative from a random one.

\subsubsection{MS MARCO reranking}

\textbf{Instruction:} \emph{``Rank the documents by how relevant each is to the question, from most to
least relevant $\dots$ Ranking: [id1], [id2], [id3], \dots''}\\
The pool is identical to the retrieval example above; what changes is the target. Every document
carries a real cross-encoder relevance score, so the gold order is defined for the entire pool
rather than only for the judged documents.\\
\textbf{Target:} the full ranking, scored by MRR@10. A HELMET-format variant renders the pool as
\texttt{[ID: n] Document: $\dots$}, asks for a \texttt{Ranking: ID3 > ID1 > \dots} permutation, and
is scored by graded NDCG@10 against cross-encoder scores bucketed to 0--3.

\texttt{[$\cdot$ 27 more documents omitted, BM25-mined from the same subset]}
\end{quote}

\subsubsection{Oolong (aggregation over labeled items)}

\textbf{Instruction:} \emph{``Read the data below and answer the question. Compute the exact answer by
analyzing every item; do not guess or approximate.''}\\
\textbf{Context (rendered verbatim, not as numbered documents; \texttt{ctx}=1024 rung shown):}
\begin{quote}\small
``The following lines contain 10 pairs of sentences, one per line. On each line, the first sentence
contains a metaphorical statement and the second sentence (separated by \texttt{<-->}) contains a
$\dots$'' \emph{[items follow; the ladder runs from 1K to 4.2M tokens]}
\end{quote}
\textbf{Query:} \emph{``In the above data, which of the labels is the most common? Give your final
answer in the form `Label: answer' where answer is one of the labels: incorrect, correct.''}\\
\textbf{Answer:} \texttt{incorrect}\quad(task group: \emph{counting}; the other groups are
\emph{user} and \emph{timeline}). The model must classify every item and then aggregate --- the
labels are not given.

\subsection{\ctc{N^2} tasks}

\subsubsection{Contradiction (PubMed)}

\textbf{Instruction:} \emph{``$\dots$identify all pairs of claims that contradict each other. A pair
of claims is contradictory if they cannot both be true at the same time. Output your answer as a JSON
list of pairs $\dots$ For example: [[1, 4], [3, 7]]''}\\
\textbf{Context (5 of 20 claims shown; $N{=}20$, $K{=}3$ gold pairs):}
\begin{quote}\small
\texttt{[2]} \emph{(gold pair with \texttt{[12]})} ``A hypertonic solution of 14.4\% has a reversible
ciliostatic effect.''\\
\texttt{[12]} \emph{(perturbed --- obvious mode)} ``A hypertonic solution of 14.4\% has an
irreversible ciliostatic effect.''\\
\texttt{[8]} \emph{(gold pair with \texttt{[11]})} ``ROS production was increased in diabetic rats
when compared to that of control rats (P $<$ 0.05), the effect of apocynin was a reduction in the ROS
production in diabetic rats (P $<$ 0.05).''\\
\texttt{[11]} \emph{(perturbed --- subtle mode)} ``In diabetic animal models, apocynin exhibited no
significant impact on the heightened reactive oxygen species generation observed relative to
non-diabetic controls (P $>$ 0.05).''\\
\texttt{[13]} \emph{(gold pair with \texttt{[17]})} ``Modulation of cell viability was assessed
exclusively through the application of exogenous IL-12 and IFN-gamma.''\\
\texttt{[$\cdot$ 15 more documents omitted; fillers come from PubMed abstracts disjoint from every
gold-source abstract in this example]}
\end{quote}
\textbf{Gold pairs:} \texttt{[[2, 12], [8, 11], [13, 17]]}. Only one sentence of each pair is
LLM-written, so writing style does not identify the planted item.

\subsubsection{Absence (derived over our corpora)}

\textbf{Instruction:} \emph{``The numbered claims above are the original corpus. The text below is a
second version of that corpus with some claims removed. Identify which of the numbered claims are
MISSING $\dots$ Missing: [id1], [id2], \dots''}\\
\textbf{Original corpus (2 of 20 claims shown):}
\begin{quote}\small
\texttt{[1]} ``The ORs decreased substantially after adjusting for socio-economic score and
integration score.''\\
\texttt{[2]} \emph{(\textbf{removed})} ``Activation of the NLRP1 and NLRP3 inflammasomes in AD was
confirmed by confocal microscopy proteins co-localization $\dots$''\\
\texttt{[$\cdot$ 18 more documents omitted]}
\end{quote}
\textbf{Second version (in the query slot; kept claims in original order):}
\begin{quote}\small
``The ORs decreased substantially after adjusting for socio-economic score and integration
score.\textbackslash n Case series study.\textbackslash n Absolute changes in HbA1c independently
predicted magnesium changes $\dots$'' \emph{(claim 2 is gone)}
\end{quote}
\textbf{Gold (removed) IDs:} \texttt{[2, 8, 20]} at deletion rate $p{=}0.1$. Scattered single
deletions are the hard regime; large contiguous gaps are easy.

\subsubsection{xabsence (cross-corpus absence)}

\textbf{Instruction:} \emph{``Below are two corpora of numbered claims, A and B. Almost every claim
has a paraphrase $\dots$ somewhere in the OTHER corpus. A few claims are UNMATCHED $\dots$ Unmatched:
[id1], [id2], \dots''}\\
\textbf{Context (5 of 19 claims shown; $P{=}8$ matched pairs, $k{=}3$ unmatched):}
\begin{quote}\small
\texttt{[1]} \emph{(corpus A, \textbf{unmatched})} ``Recently, we found that Kupffer cells isolated
from rats treated only once with ethanol were sensitized to endotoxin 24 hr later.''\\
\texttt{[3]} \emph{(corpus A, \textbf{unmatched})} ``Since all residents promptly passed the ABS
examinations, it was not possible to discern factors associated with Board performances $\dots$''\\
\texttt{[2]} \emph{(corpus A, matched)} ``Clinical and biochemical data and the alterations in livers
resected at transplantation (n=7) or at autopsy (n=2) were gathered $\dots$''\\
\texttt{[4]} \emph{(corpus A, matched)} ``Twelve infants less than 3 months of age, with severe
respiratory distress.''\\
\texttt{[14]} \emph{(corpus B, \textbf{unmatched})} ``RD2 also correlated with neuropsychological
outcome 1 year after aSAH.''\\
\texttt{[$\cdot$ 14 more documents omitted; the B block holds a low-overlap paraphrase of each
matched A claim]}
\end{quote}
\textbf{Gold (unmatched) IDs:} \texttt{[1, 3, 14]}. Paraphrase twins are filtered to word-Jaccard
$\leq 0.3$, so surface string matching cannot find the orphans.

\subsection{\ctc{N^3} tasks and beyond}

\subsubsection{Cycle detection}

\textbf{Query:} \emph{``Each claim asserts a strict comparison (the first person ranks strictly above
the second). Find every set of claims that forms a cycle --- an impossible loop where the ranking
comes back to where it started.''}\\
\textbf{Context (5 of 20 claims shown; one predicate throughout: ``\emph{X scored higher on the exam
than Y}''):}
\begin{quote}\small
\texttt{[2]} \emph{(gold)} ``Gita scored higher on the exam than Hugo''\\
\texttt{[16]} \emph{(gold)} ``Bruno scored higher on the exam than Gita''\\
\texttt{[19]} \emph{(gold)} ``Hugo scored higher on the exam than Bruno''\\
\texttt{[1]} \emph{(background)} ``Zara scored higher on the exam than Dina''\\
\texttt{[3]} \emph{(background)} ``Kane scored higher on the exam than Bea''\\
\texttt{[$\cdot$ 15 more claims omitted; all background edges point forward in one global entity

\subsubsection{Textgroups}

\textbf{Query:} \emph{``Each passage has a value: the number of nouns. Find every group of 3 passages
whose values add up to 70 (exactly 70).''}\\
\textbf{Context (3 of 20 passages shown; the value is a property of the prose, never printed):}
\begin{quote}\small

\end{quote}
\textbf{Gold groups:} \texttt{[[1, 11, 12], [2, 14, 17]]} --- noun counts $34{+}31{+}5 = 70$ and

passage's count is unambiguous, and sentence structure is varied so the count is not a proxy for
