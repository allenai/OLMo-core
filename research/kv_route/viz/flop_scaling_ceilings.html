Where the Router Speedup Runs Out

The 40x question priced in wall-clock rather than FLOPs, re-measured after a bug left a third of the FFN unrouted. Every number below is a real forward+backward step on one H200; the trainable ceiling and the deployment-only ceiling are kept apart throughout.

The answer

measured · trainable
6.46x
the best all-learned configuration, 4B at 16k against a 1419 ms dense control: head gating, routed FFN and compacted block skip at keep=1/16. The defensible operating point — keep=0.25 — is 4.09x. 27B at 8k reaches 5.10x.
measured · deployment only
8.39x
the same operating point with the always-null FFN layers deleted rather than routed. This is not available during training. It is faster only because it stops issuing router kernels: 6,740 launches against 9,886. 27B: 6.77x.
measured · packed
4.22x
the same 4B recipe with the sequence packed into 32 documents, which is how real training runs. Packing already makes attention cheap, so it roughly halves the prize: 6.46x → 4.22x trainable, 8.39x → 5.08x with deletion. Plan against this number.
verdict
20x is not reachable
the honest best is 8.39x. Zeroing GDN entirely gives 17.0x; zeroing GDN and attention gives 21.1x; the un-routable floor at that keep is 22.4x. So 20x needs both mixers at zero, at a keep of 1/16 that is not a trainable model.
These are pinned-operating-point costs, not achieved training speedups. Every number on this page is what the hardware does when the token distribution is forced to a given sparsity. A parallel real-training run shows that a learned router under a budget hinge does not reach its target sparsity inside a short (31-step) SFT epoch, so a training job that merely switches these routers on will not realise these numbers. Read them as the wall-clock price of an operating point — an upper bound on what routing could pay if a router got there — and never as a measured training speedup.
Read keep ≤ 0.125 as a mechanism probe, not an operating point. Every routed block is forced to skip the same tokens, so at keep=1/16 the 93.75% that are skipped do no mixing in any block — they ride the residual stream from embedding to head. The wall-clock is honest (the compacted path's cost depends on how many tokens are kept, not which), but it is not a model anyone would train. The defensible trainable point is keep=0.25: 4.09x at 4B/16k.

The ceiling is not a fixed number — it rises as you skip harder

The earlier reading of this study was that a floor of roughly 8.7% of every step caps the family at about 11.5x. That is correct at dense, and it reproduces independently at both scales. It is not a ceiling on an aggressively skipped model, because compaction removes skipped tokens from the block shell too — the two RMSNorms and two residual adds each block runs over the whole activation stream. Cut the kept fraction and the floor itself shrinks, so dense / floor climbs.

All measured, by the same cumulative-ablation method on each arm at its own operating point: 11.6x at dense and keep=0.5, 16.7x at keep=0.25, 22.4x at keep=1/16 for 4B/16k. 27B/8k traces the same curve from a completely different component mix: 11.6 / 12.3 / 17.8 / 20.9x.

The joint sweep: every lever at once

Head gating on throughout, block skip compacted and fixed-capacity, KV router excluded except for one arm that keeps it to re-confirm the verdict. Measured wall-clock against the calculated FLOP cut, both axes logarithmic. Three things read straight off the panels: the trainable curves saturate — keep 0.25 → 0.125 buys 1.44x, 0.125 → 1/16 buys 1.09x for another 2× less work; the three f_null curves sit almost on top of each other at low keep, so the FFN router stops mattering once block skip is aggressive (2.7% at keep=1/16, and in the wrong direction); and the static-deletion arms sit above every trainable one at the same FLOPs.

The KV router loses for the third independent time. At 4B it takes 4.043x down to 3.948x while raising the FLOP cut from 19.8x to 25.3x; at 27B, 3.123x → 3.046x. Compacted block skip already removes a skipped token as a key by gathering rather than masking, so the KV router re-does that work and charges for it. Keep it out of the stack.

Packing roughly halves the prize. Same 4B, same 16k, sequence packed into 32 documents — how real training runs. The dense step falls 1419 ms → 1120 ms because packing already makes attention cheap, and the recipe falls with it: best trainable 6.46x → 4.22x, best with static deletion 8.39x → 5.08x, with GPU busy at the best arm down to 37.5%.

The bottleneck moved: it is GDN now, not the FFN

The step partition re-measured on the routed model, with the routers live at each arm's operating point. One stage is new relative to earlier versions of this study: delete the block itself. Replacing a block's mixer and FFN leaves the block shell running — two RMSNorms and two residual adds per block over the whole activation stream, forward and backward, 32 times. That is per-token GPU work no component ablation ever removed, and it is essentially the entire floor (8.4% of a dense 4B step, with the true base measuring 0.0% underneath it).

The blockers are host synchronisations and value-dependent shapes, and they are enumerable: the fused-linear CE kernel (which falls away once the head is gated below the unfused threshold); head gating as shipped (lm_head.py:286 bool(keep.any()) plus x[keep], whose output shape depends on tensor values — replacing it with a fixed row budget chosen by topk makes the whole 4B/16k step capturable and is exact for capacity ≥ supervised rows); the block-skip holder's bookkeeping, three host syncs in block_skip.py, which is what still blocks every routed arm; and the FFN router's bincount(…).tolist(). None is load-bearing numerics — they are budget statistics and dispatch counts that could live on device and be read once every N steps. That is the concrete engineering item, and by the idle column it is worth about 1.19x at the recommended operating point.

So: is 20x reachable?

Not in practice. All against the same 1419.0 ms dense control, 4B at 16k, batch 1, one H200, in one process. Solid bars are measured; the faded, dash-outlined bars are calculated by subtracting a measured component from a measured step.

dense control / floor / targetmeasured, trainablemeasured, deployment onlycalculated

What stops us is GDN. It is 53% of the best arm's step at 4B and 34.8% at 27B, and it is flat from keep=0.25 to keep=1/16 — skipping tokens stops helping it. Every GDN lever tried in this study is already refuted: q/k/v fusion 1.02x, chunk size 1–2%, packing 0.91x, expand_v about 1.4% of a step, more batch and head parallelism refuted. Even a perfect GDN gives 17.0x. Reaching 20x needs GDN at zero and attention at zero, at a keep of 1/16 that is not a trainable model.

Plan against 4–6.5x trainable and 5–8.4x with post-training FFN deletion, halved by packing (4.22x / 5.08x measured at --docs 32). The next real 1.19x is CUDA graphs, blocked by four enumerable host syncs rather than by anything fundamental.
