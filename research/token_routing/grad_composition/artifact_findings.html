• Split before block 7–8; documents can leave by about block 24. Document tokens' share of the hidden-state gradient is flat through block ~8 and then falls: mt4 dense after 366 steps 0.56 at block 7 → 0.41 at 12 → 0.25 at 16 → 0.11 at 24 (base 0.15 → 0.06). Keeping 10% of each document from block L on, ranked by the true gradient, retains a smoothly rising share of parameter-gradient energy: 0.91 / 0.93 / 0.96 / 0.99 at L = 1 / 4 / 7 / 12 (trained; random keep at 7: 0.85). There is no step at block 7 in where the gradient lives, so the training-time threshold at 7 is more likely about how well hL predicts importance than about the gradient itself.
  • One keep decision is not enough after training; rescore once at ~16. Body-token rankings decorrelate with depth and with training: Spearman(G7, G16) 0.78 → 0.60 and (G7, G24) 0.57 → 0.35, base → dense 366 (mt4 rows). The 10% kept at block 7 hold 0.83 → 0.74 of block 16's gradient energy. A pyramid of 50% at 3, 10% at 7 and 3% at 16 holds 0.69 of block 24's energy, against 0.61 for a single 3% cut at block 7. The 50% stage at block 3 is nearly free: it holds 0.92–0.96 of every later block's energy.
  • Gradient and activation targets disagree everywhere, and activation loses. Spearman(Gb, ‖hb+1−hb‖) is 0.0–0.2 at every block; (G7, ‖h7−h0‖) is mostly slightly negative (−0.37 to +0.15 by task, most negative on strmatch, reorder and xabsence). Forward ablation, 10% per document before block 7: the gradient keep costs ΔCE +0.13 (base) / +0.30 (dense 366), the activation keep +0.47 / +0.36, random +0.35 / +0.52. Answer-attention mass at the late full-attention blocks agrees moderately (ρ 0.45–0.65). It matches the gradient keep on the base but loses after training (+0.49 vs +0.30; strmatch on mt11: +0.91 vs +0.53).
  • Prasann's question, important-but-learned tokens. Training does pull the gradient keep away from the attention keep: the share of the late-attention top 10% that a G7 keep misses rises from 0.37 to 0.56. But the missed tokens sit at the 64th–82nd percentile of G7, not near zero, and keeping by attention instead is worse in the ablation. Tokens are not being dropped for being learned. The real risk is the depth instability above.
  • Early blocks hold a large, shrinking share of the parameter gradient. Blocks 0–6 hold 0.45 of the ‖g‖² at the base (block 0 alone 0.17), 0.20 per row and 0.29–0.34 for the summed minibatch gradient after 366 mt4 steps, and 0.33 on mt11 after 330. Training blocks 0–6 only on full-context steps gives up a fifth to a third of the gradient energy, and nearly half early in training.
  • strmatch is incompressible at 10% per document. Its rows have 82 documents of ~25 body tokens, so a 10% per-document keep leaves ~3 tokens each, mostly header. The gradient is spread over all documents rather than gold (gold enrichment 2.2× at base, 0.9× trained). A per-document top 10% captures 0.68 of block 7's energy, vs 0.92 for a row-level top 10%. Its rankings are the least stable with depth (G7~G24 0.26–0.38). After 330 mt11 steps, even the oracle gradient keep costs ΔCE +0.53 at a full CE of 0.009 (random +0.95). Short documents need a token floor or row-level allocation.
  • grouping is not explained by gradient composition. At the oracle level it compresses easily: ablation ΔCE −0.00 for the gradient keep (random +0.15), split fidelity 0.98. It does differ in two ways: every document is gold, and document tokens keep receiving gradient deep in the stack (0.27 at block 16, 0.14 at 24). The training-time loss more likely comes from the learned scorer on all-gold rows, or from the kept tokens' gradients being redistributed. Test with grad_fidelity.py on grouping rows.
  • Merge rather than drop, at the span level. What a 10% keep drops is spread out: the top 10% of dropped tokens hold only 0.26–0.42 of the dropped mass, so there is no small set to rescue. But importance is smooth along the document (lag-1 correlation of log G7 0.5–0.85), so pooling runs of neighbouring dropped tokens should lose little.
  • The router-trained checkpoint has adapted to its own drop. At the same CE as dense (0.118 vs 0.112), its forward barely notices a 10% keep at block 7 (+0.02 vs +0.30). Its gradient also stays earlier-heavy and more stable: blocks 0–6 hold 0.32 vs 0.20, and the block-7 keep holds 0.84 vs 0.74 of block 16. The router ran at LR 2e-6 and dense at 5e-6; the LR 2e-6 dense control is running.
